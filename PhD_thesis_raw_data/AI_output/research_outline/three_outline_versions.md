## Version 1: Balanced overview

Complex abelian varieties provide a setting in which algebraic geometry interacts closely with topology and representation theory. The aim of this doctoral project is to understand the geometry of their subvarieties through tangent Gauss maps and conormal cycles. Particular attention will be paid to the irreducibility, dimension, and homology classes of subvarieties obtained by adding points in fibres of the conormal Gauss map. These constructions give a geometric approach to cycles arising from the convolution of perverse sheaves and the associated Tannaka groups.

For a subvariety of an abelian variety, the tangent Gauss map records how its tangent spaces vary after translation to the origin. The conormal Gauss map records the invariant covectors annihilating these tangent spaces. When the latter map is generically finite, its monodromy describes the permutations of the points in a general fibre. Taking weighted sums of these points produces new subvarieties, whose geometry depends on the monodromy action. The project studies both this action and the geometric invariants of the resulting subvarieties.

The work completed so far establishes a geometric framework for these constructions and provides monodromy calculations for curves in Jacobian and Prym varieties. It also gives explicit formulas for the Chern-Mather classes of the constructed subvarieties when the conormal monodromy is the full symmetric group, or, in the symmetric case, the full signed symmetric group. The formulas combine Pontryagin products with combinatorial coefficients obtained from partitions. They allow dimensions and fundamental homology classes to be recovered and yield concrete descriptions in the Jacobian case.

Recent counterexamples require a refinement of the broader conjectural picture. A curve in a simple abelian threefold can have a tangent Gauss map of degree three without any nontrivial ambient automorphism preserving the curve and acting by a scalar on tangent spaces. A separate construction involving Schoen surfaces shows that the two half-spin cycles associated with type D monodromy can have different homology classes. These examples reveal geometric information not captured by the previously proposed symmetry principles and provide starting points for further investigation.

The next stage has three connected objectives. First, I will seek sufficient geometric conditions for generic injectivity of the tangent Gauss map and for large conormal monodromy, using linear systems on curves, Jacobian quotients, and Prym constructions. Second, I will investigate how the existing characteristic-class formulas can be extended to type D monodromy while retaining the distinction between the two half-spin orbits. Third, I will use explicit examples and degenerations to study which features determine the dimensions and homology classes of the resulting supports. The question of equality of the half-spin support dimensions will be treated separately from the disproved equality of their homology classes.

The methods combine the geometry of curves and abelian varieties, covering-space monodromy, intersection theory, and the combinatorics of Weyl group actions. Explicit symbolic calculations will support the analysis of examples. The intended outcome is a collection of precise criteria and computable invariants that explains both the established positive results and the newly identified exceptional behaviour.

## Version 2: Gauss maps and revised geometric questions

The central question of this doctoral project is how much the tangent spaces of a subvariety determine its geometry inside a complex abelian variety. Translation identifies all ambient tangent spaces with a fixed vector space, giving the tangent Gauss map. For a curve, this map assigns its tangent direction to each smooth point. Understanding its degree and ramification is closely related to understanding the monodromy of the conormal Gauss map and, through characteristic cycles, the representation theory associated with the curve.

The initial motivation was the expectation that a failure of generic injectivity should be explained by ambient symmetries, such as translations or reflections. Preliminary work investigated this expectation for curves in Jacobian and Prym varieties, where the Gauss map can be described using canonical and Prym-canonical linear systems. This led to positive results for specific families, together with a study of how automorphisms, second fundamental forms, and curvature constrain the fibres of the tangent Gauss map.

A recent counterexample shows that the proposed symmetry explanation fails even for a simple ambient abelian variety. Its construction uses a non-hyperelliptic curve X of genus four with a theta characteristic L having two independent sections. A simple three-dimensional quotient A of its Jacobian is chosen so that the invariant one-forms pulled back from A form the space of products of sections of L. The induced tangent Gauss map factors as a degree-three map to the projective line followed by the quadratic Veronese embedding. Its image curve in A has no nontrivial preserving ambient automorphism with scalar differential. Thus the geometry of the relevant linear system can produce repeated tangent directions without the predicted symmetry.

The project will use this example to develop more precise questions about the possible degrees and factorizations of tangent Gauss maps. I will study how subspaces of canonical forms arising from abelian quotients interact with pencils on curves and with the rational Hodge structures defining those quotients. Initial attention will be given to low-genus curves and to families accessible through Prym constructions. The aim is to find sufficient conditions for generic injectivity and to identify mechanisms producing higher degree. The role of ramification and the variation of the second fundamental form along Gauss fibres will also be examined.

These questions have consequences for conormal monodromy. A factorization of the tangent Gauss map can impose a block structure on a general conormal fibre. I will determine this structure in explicit families and study how it affects the irreducible components of subvarieties formed by weighted sums of fibre points. Existing formulas for Chern-Mather classes in the full symmetric and full signed symmetric cases provide a basis for computing their dimensions and homology classes.

The broader comparison will include the Schoen-surface example, where two half-spin cycles have unequal homology classes despite arising from closely related monodromy orbits. Together, these investigations aim to clarify which geometric conclusions follow from monodromy and which require additional information about the embedding. The expected results are refined injectivity and monodromy criteria, explicit exceptional families, and applications to the geometry of convolution constructions on abelian varieties.

## Version 3: Characteristic classes and the type D problem

This doctoral project studies subvarieties of complex abelian varieties through the conormal cycles associated with them. The main objective is to compute the dimensions and homology classes of new subvarieties produced by convolution and to explain their relation to monodromy and representation theory. Characteristic cycles provide the connection: they translate constructions involving perverse sheaves into conic Lagrangian cycles in the cotangent bundle, where the group law of the abelian variety permits a direct geometric description.

Let Z be a subvariety whose conormal Gauss map is generically finite. A general fibre determines finitely many points of Z. Taking their weighted sums and varying the covector produces subvarieties denoted by Z^(m), with irreducible components governed by monodromy orbits. The dimension and fundamental class of each support can be recovered from the Segre classes of its conormal cycle, expressed in the Chern-Mather convention used in the preliminary work. This makes characteristic-class calculations a practical tool for studying these otherwise implicit geometric constructions.

The work completed so far gives explicit formulas when the monodromy is the full symmetric group and, for symmetric subvarieties, the full signed symmetric group. These formulas express the relevant classes in terms of Pontryagin products of the original classes, with coefficients obtained by combinatorial inversion on partitions. Applications to curves in their Jacobians yield explicit dimension formulas. Monodromy calculations for Abel-Prym curves provide further settings in which the method applies.

The next principal problem is the case of the even signed symmetric group, the Weyl group of type D. Here the signed sums of points in a general Gauss fibre split into two orbits according to the parity of the number of minus signs. These are related to the two half-spin representations and define two conic Lagrangian cycles. A recent construction using Schoen surfaces in abelian fourfolds shows that their homology classes need not agree: the example has conormal Gauss degree eight, monodromy W(D4), and distinct degree-four homology components of the two cycles. Consequently, a type D calculation must retain information that distinguishes the two orbits.

I will investigate how to compute the sum and the difference of these two classes separately. Degeneration to a union built from a genus-two Jacobian and a product of curves provides an explicit setting for studying specialization. Particular care will be given to the multiplicities of the summation maps and to contributions over loci where the Gauss map is not finite. The goal is to determine which additional geometric or Hodge-theoretic data are needed beyond the formulas already available in types A and C.

A second objective is to study the dimensions of the two projected supports. Their possible equality is a separate question, since unequal homology classes do not imply unequal dimensions. More generally, I will examine how changes in monodromy affect irreducibility and dimension, using the degree-three tangent Gauss counterexample to test the limits of large-monodromy assumptions. The intended outcome is an extension of the existing computational framework to selected type D cases, explicit formulas or obstructions for half-spin classes, and a clearer account of the geometric information carried by characteristic cycles.

## Editorial notes

- Version 1 is the most balanced application text. Version 2 supplies a sharper motivation for revising the original conjectures. Version 3 gives the most concrete intersection-theoretic next step.
- The counterexample reports have been used as supplied; drafting these outlines does not constitute an independent verification of all their proofs or external citations.
- The curve report establishes an immersive normalization of its image. It does not assert that the image curve is a smooth embedded curve, and the drafts do not make that stronger claim.
- The half-spin report disproves equality of homology classes. It does not establish unequal support dimensions. It concerns a type D extension beyond the conjectures explicitly displayed on the poster.
- The proposed future objectives are suggestions inferred from the supplied work. No research timetable, promised classification theorem, or new completed result has been added.
